## What a result card shows

One card per hotel. On it:

- The **cheapest total for the whole stay**, in your selling currency, with your margin applied.
  Not a per-night rate. The line above the list says so explicitly: *"Prices are the total for 3
  nights, in EUR."*
- Whether that rate is **Free cancellation** or **Non-refundable**, and — when the supplier states
  it — the date the free-cancellation window closes.
- The star rating, where the hotel has one.
- Amounts payable at the hotel rather than to the supplier, labelled *payable at the hotel*, beside
  the terms of the rate they belong to.

## Choosing a room on the card

Under the hotel's name is one row per **class** — the board and the cancellation terms — stating the
two prices that hotel is sold between and how many room names that spread covers, such as
*4 room names*.

Press a row to open it. The portal asks the supplier for that hotel's rooms at those terms and lists
them, each with a checkbox, its price and who is selling it. That is a fresh call, so it happens
when you press and not before — and a row can legitimately open onto *No room is on sale at these
terms any more*.

Ticking rooms builds an **offer** you can send a customer as a PDF — see
[Preparing an offer](/en/search/preparing-an-offer/).

The **↗** at the end of the row opens the hotel's own page on that class, where you book. It is a
real link, so middle-click it to compare two hotels in two tabs.

A re-price is a fresh call to the supplier, so a rate can be gone by the time the page opens. It
says so and shows the cheapest instead, rather than quietly putting a different room under the
terms you picked.

## Why one offer and not another

Within a group of genuinely comparable offers, OneRate picks the cheapest for you. Two offers are
comparable only if the hotel, board, refundability, cancellation deadline, occupancy and currency
all match — see [comparable groups](/en/what-is-onerate/#the-one-idea-to-take-away-comparable-groups).

Offers that are *not* comparable are not ranked against each other. They are shown as separate
options, and you choose.

### "Why this supplier?"

Every automatic selection records its reasoning, and the link on the card opens it: which offers
were considered, which were excluded and why, and the deciding difference.

Use it when a supplier asks why you booked elsewhere, when a colleague queries the ranking, or when
a price looks wrong. It is a record of what the portal actually saw, not a reconstruction.

### Cancellation deadlines you should not trust blindly

Some suppliers state a cancellation deadline without stating a timezone. OneRate resolves the
deadline in the **hotel's** timezone and marks it:

> Estimate — this supplier states no time zone, so this is the earliest the deadline could fall.

That is the conservative reading, on purpose. Treat an estimated deadline as the last safe moment,
not as a promise.

## Filters and sorting

Filters apply to what is already on screen; they do not re-run the search.

- **Free cancellation only**
- **Board** — room only, bed and breakfast, half board, and so on
- **Max price** in your selling currency
- **Star rating**, with *and up*
- **Hotel name**

Every choice carries the number of hotels it would leave you with, so you can see what a narrower
filter costs before you apply it. The name box says how many its text matches.

The count updates as you filter — *"12 of 48 hotels"* — and **Clear filters** puts it back.
**Clear filters** is always there, greyed out when there is nothing to clear, so the column does
not move under your pointer the moment you use it. If a filter empties the list, the portal says
how many results the search actually found, so you can see it was the filter and not the search.

:::note
Hotels with **no star rating** are excluded by *any* star choice, and the screen says how many. An
unrated hotel is not a zero-star hotel; it is a hotel whose rating the supplier did not send.
:::

Sorting: **Recommended** (the default ranking), **Price low to high**, **Price high to low**, or
**Hotel name A–Z**.

## List and map

**List** and **Map** show the same results.

The map draws on **Google Maps**, on OneRate's own key — nothing to configure, nothing billed to
you. Your agency can switch it to OpenStreetMap in [Settings](/en/manage/settings/). If OneRate
cannot draw Google for a while, the map falls back to OpenStreetMap and tells you it did.

Hotels with **no recorded location** cannot be placed. The map says how many are missing so you do
not mistake the map for the whole result set. Pins that overlap cluster; zoom in to separate them.

## Held rankings

Results keep arriving after the first ones appear, and prices can move. If the portal reordered the
list underneath you, you would click a different hotel from the one you meant.

So it holds the list and tells you instead:

> 4 new results — the list is held while you read

Press **Update the list** when you are ready. Nothing is lost while it is held; nothing changes
until you ask.

## Offers in another currency

Your agency has one selling currency. Offers priced in it are the main list.

Offers a supplier priced in **another** currency appear in a separate **Other currency** section,
ranked only against each other. The note on that section is the important part:

> OneRate does not convert between currencies, so they are not ranked against the list above — only
> against each other, within one currency.

These offers are bookable. You just have to understand what you are booking: the amount shown is
what the supplier charges in its own currency, and what it costs you in your currency depends on
your bank, not on OneRate. See [Pricing](/en/manage/pricing/).

## Opening a hotel

Selecting a hotel opens its own page, which re-prices the stay against the supplier at that moment
and shows every rate option with photos and detail.

That page reconstructs itself from its address, so you can middle-click a hotel to open it in a new
tab, or send the link to a colleague — they will get the same stay, priced when they open it.

If the rate is gone by then, the page says so: *"This hotel has no rates for these dates right
now."* Go back and search again.
