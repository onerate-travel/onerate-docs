**My account** is your own account, not your agency's. Every role has one, customers included, and
nothing here is gated on what your role may do.

## Identity

Your name, email address and role, as your agency sees them.

**Display name** is the only one you can change here — it is what your colleagues see next to your
actions. Your email address and your role are set by whoever invited you; ask them to change either.

## Password

Change it with your **current password** plus a new one of at least **12 characters**.

Changing your password **signs out all your other sessions**. That is the point: it is what you do
when you think someone else may have your password, and it would be useless if their session
survived it.

If you have forgotten your current password, sign out and use
[Forgot your password?](/en/start/signing-in/#forgotten-password) instead.

## Two-factor authentication

Shown as **On** or **Off**.

**Required** for agency owners — and for OneRate's own platform staff. Optional for admins, agents
and customers, and worth turning on: your OneRate account can spend your agency's supplier
contracts.

### Turning it on

**Set up two-factor authentication**, then confirm your password, add the key to an authenticator
app, and enter the generated code. Full walkthrough in
[Signing in](/en/start/signing-in/#two-step-verification).

### Backup codes

**Generate new backup codes** replaces your existing set. Confirm your password first.

**The old codes stop working immediately.** Save the new ones before you close the dialog — they are
shown once and cannot be retrieved afterwards. A password manager is the right place; the phone
that holds your authenticator is not, since losing that phone is the situation they exist for.

### Turning it off

Possible only if it is not required for your role. Confirm your password.

An agency owner cannot turn it off. There is no setting for it and no support request that changes
it — the account that manages the subscription and the supplier credentials keeps its second
factor.

## Active sessions

Everywhere you are currently signed in: device, IP address, when the session began, when it
expires. Your current one is marked **This device**.

**Sign out** ends any session that is not your current one.

Do it when:

- you signed in on a shared or public machine and left,
- you have lost a device,
- or you see a session you do not recognise — in which case change your password too, which ends
  them all at once.

If it is your only session, the screen says so rather than showing you a button that would sign you
out of the screen you are reading.

Signing a device out asks you first. It cannot be undone: whoever is using it is signed out at
once and has to sign in again.

## When your session ends

A session does not last forever, and your agency can make it shorter (see
[security postures](/en/manage/settings/#security-postures)).

When it ends, the portal takes you back to the sign-in page and says why. Signing in returns you to
the screen you were on — not to the home page.

**Unless you were in the middle of something.** If a form has something typed into it — a supplier
credential, a guest's name on a booking review, a setting you were editing — the portal does *not*
navigate away. It shows a bar at the top with a **Sign in again** button, and leaves what you typed
on screen until you press it.

Two messages are worth telling apart:

- *"Your session ended"* — sign in again and carry on.
- *"Your access to this agency is suspended"* — signing in changes nothing. Somebody at that agency
  switched your access off. If you work in another agency, the switcher at the top takes you there;
  otherwise, speak to that agency's owner or admin.

## A short security routine

1. Turn on two-factor authentication even if your role does not require it.
2. Keep your backup codes somewhere that is not the phone with the authenticator on it.
3. Glance at active sessions occasionally.
4. When a colleague leaves, remove them from [Team](/en/manage/team/) the same day — their session
   is otherwise as valid as yours.
