---
title: Supplier preferences
description: Overriding cheapest-first ranking — pinning a supplier, or preferring one unless another is meaningfully cheaper.
---

By default the cheapest offer wins within each comparable group. That is right until your agency
has a volume commitment, a better back-end deal, or a supplier whose service is worth a few euros.

**Preferences** is where you say so. Owners and admins.

## The two rule types

### Always prefer

Pin a supplier. Where that supplier has an offer in a comparable group, it wins — regardless of
price.

Use it when a contract genuinely requires it. Understand what you are giving up: you will pay more
sometimes, and the [Why this supplier?](/en/search/reading-results/#why-this-supplier) record will
show exactly when and by how much.

### Prefer unless pricier

The softer and usually better rule. Prefer a supplier **unless another is more than *n*% cheaper.**

> Stay with RateHawk unless another supplier is more than 3% cheaper.

You hit your volume target on the ordinary bookings and still catch the ones where staying loyal
would cost real money. Enter the percentage between 0 and 100.

## Scope

Each rule is either:

- **All destinations** — leave the destination field blank, or
- **One destination** — enter the destination code, for example `IST`.

A destination rule applies only to searches for that destination. This is how you follow a contract
that is regional rather than global.

## Adding a rule

1. **Preferences → Add a rule.**
2. Choose the **rule type**.
3. Choose the **supplier**.
4. Optionally enter a **destination**; leave blank for all.
5. For *Prefer unless pricier*, enter the **percentage**.
6. **Add rule.**

The list shows every rule with its type, supplier and scope. **Remove** deletes one and asks you to
confirm which.

## With no rules at all

> No preference rules. The cheapest comparable offer wins by default.

That is a perfectly good place to be. Add rules when you have a commercial reason, not
pre-emptively.

## What preferences do not change

- **They do not merge incomparable offers.** A pinned supplier's half-board rate still does not win
  against another supplier's room-only rate — they were never in the same group. See
  [comparable groups](/en/what-is-onerate/#the-one-idea-to-take-away-comparable-groups).
- **They do not hide anything.** Other suppliers' offers are still shown, still reachable through
  the options expander, and still bookable if you choose one.
- **They do not silently override you.** Every automatic selection a rule caused is recorded in the
  **Why this supplier?** trail, with the price difference it accepted.

That last point is the reason to prefer the softer rule. A pin that quietly costs €40 a booking is
invisible in a total and obvious in the trail — but only if somebody looks. A percentage rule puts
the limit in the rule itself.

## Reviewing them

Preferences are commercial decisions with an expiry date: contracts change, volume targets are met,
suppliers get worse. Nothing in the portal will remind you.

Read them alongside your [reports](/en/manage/reports/) — the **By supplier** breakdown is the
number that tells you whether a rule is doing what you set it up to do.
